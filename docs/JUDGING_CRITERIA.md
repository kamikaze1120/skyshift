# Judging Criteria — Foundation for Every Design Decision

Every feature built for Skyshift must earn its place by moving at least one of these eight criteria. This is the project's north star; when in doubt, check a decision against this table before building it.

1. **Science** — best and most valid use of science and/or scientific methods.
2. **Data access** — best makes space data accessible, or leverages it for a unique application.
3. **Innovation** — most innovative use of technology. This means **no hallucinations while building**: real data only, nothing fabricated.
4. **Impact** — most potential to improve life on Earth or in the universe.
5. **Plausibility** — most plausible concept and design.
6. **Storytelling** — captures hearts; creatively communicates the potential of open data through storytelling.
7. **Global connection** — best connects people around the world through technology.
8. **Craft** — most effectively combines technical and creative skills.

## What wins vs. loses each criterion

| Criterion | What wins it | What loses it |
|---|---|---|
| Science | Real epoch timestamps, correct coordinates, a proper "blink" comparison like the method used to discover Pluto, clear notes on uncertainty | Pretty images with no scientific meaning |
| Data access | A beginner finds a moving object in under 30 seconds with no astronomy knowledge | Recreating IRSA's expert interface |
| Innovation / no hallucinations | Every pixel traces back to a SPHEREx file, with its ID and DOI one click away. AI only explains; it never generates sky data | AI-generated "enhanced" images, or invented objects |
| Impact | Citizen science: anyone can flag a candidate mover for follow-up | "It's educational," stated without a mechanism |
| Plausibility | Works on real Quick Release data, with honest coverage limits | Promising features the data can't support yet |
| Storytelling | A guided first experience: "This brown dwarf moved X arcseconds while you were living your last six months" | A README and a search box |
| Global connection | A shared public map of what people around the world have flagged | A single-player tool |
| Craft | Solid data pipeline plus a beautiful, emotional interface | Strong on one side only |

Where most teams will already be competitive: **data access** and **plausibility**. Where a SPHEREx time-lapse tool either stands out or looks like every other image viewer: **storytelling, science, and global connection**. Weight effort accordingly.
